# EnduranceIQ

A smart second opinion on your marathon training. EnduranceIQ reads your
watch data and tells you what you're doing wrong, why it matters, and
what to change.

**Live:** [endriq.vercel.app](https://endriq.vercel.app)

> **Developers / contributors:** see [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md)
> for local setup, env vars, database migrations, and deployment.

---

## What it does

Most self-coached marathon runners train too hard on their easy days and
don't know it. Wearables show the data — they don't interpret it.

EnduranceIQ connects to your Strava account, analyzes your weekly
training, and delivers a plain-language report every week with
evidence-backed findings and specific recommendations.

---

## Features

### Weekly training report
Every week you get a full analysis of your training:

- **Intensity check** — Compares your week to the 80/20 research target.
  Flags when easy runs aren't actually easy.
- **Training load** — Plain-language status: *Normal, Elevated, Spike,
  Low*. Based on your 4-week rolling baseline.
- **Findings** — Rules engine covering 6 patterns: intensity
  distribution, load spike, no rest day, long run pace, low cadence
  on intervals, and concurrent training interference.
- **LLM narrative** — Three-section weekly summary (*Went well / Needs
  work / Next week*) generated by Claude Haiku from your structured
  training metrics.
- **Per-session feedback** — Each session has an expandable Explain
  panel with four fields: Observation, Comparison, Suggestion, and
  Why this status.
- **Trend sparklines** — 8-week rolling charts for distance, training
  load, and easy-zone share.

### Strength recommendations *(experimental)*
Pattern-driven weekly session based on what your running data shows.
Selects exercises by running weakness — plyometrics for low cadence,
posterior chain for long run drift, single-leg economy for low easy
load, maintenance near race day. Scheduling avoids the 6-hour
interference window before quality runs.

Gated behind an opt-in flag (Settings → Experimental features) until
reviewed by a qualified S&C coach. All citations link to verified DOIs.

### Data integrations
- **Strava** — OAuth 2.0, 90-day historical pull, webhook for
  automatic sync on new activities.
- **COROS** — One-click OAuth, manual sync directly from your COROS
  watch without going through Strava.

### Language support
Full EN / ID toggle in the nav. Indonesian uses casual *lo-gue* register.
The weekly LLM narrative is automatically translated to Bahasa for ID
users. Share card images stay in English regardless of locale.

### Roast mode *(opt-in)*
A sarcastic version of the weekly LLM narrative — dry British
observational humour, not cruelty. Roasts the training patterns, not
the person. Enable in Settings → Experimental features. Never shown
on share cards.

### Share cards
1080×1080 PNG cards for Instagram Stories, Threads, or WhatsApp.
Share any weekly report directly from the report page.

### Profile completeness
A dismissible banner surfaces any missing profile fields (sex, race
date, weekly km, HR values) on the dashboard and report, with direct
links to update them in Settings.

### Evidence citations
Every recommendation cites its source. All DOIs verified. Full
methodology at [endriq.vercel.app/learn](https://endriq.vercel.app/learn).

## Getting started

1. **Sign up** at [endriq.vercel.app](https://endriq.vercel.app)
2. **Complete onboarding** — race type, goal date, weekly km, sex,
   and optionally max HR and resting HR
3. **Connect Strava** in Settings → Integrations
4. **Sync** your last 90 days → open your weekly report

Reports refresh automatically via Strava webhook after the initial sync.

## Methodology

Full write-up at [/learn](https://endriq.vercel.app/learn). Key sources:

- **Intensity distribution** — Seiler (2010); Stöggl & Sperlich (2014)
- **Training load (ACWR)** — Gabbett (2016); Hulin et al. (2016)
- **Concurrent training** — Fyfe et al. (2014); Wilson et al. (2012)
- **Strength for runners** — Beattie et al. (2017); Blagrove et al. (2018)
- **TRIMP** — Banister (1991); Seiler & Kjerland (2006)
